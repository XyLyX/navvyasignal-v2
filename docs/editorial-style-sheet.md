# NavvyaSignal draft conventions

One page. Pass/fail. If a draft fails a gate, it does not publish.

## 1. Title

- **Rule:** The title states the judgment or the "so what." A topic label is a fail.
- **Test:** Can a reader tell what you concluded without reading the post? If no, rewrite.
- **Bad:** "Assessing Strait of Hormuz Tensions". **Good:** "Iran's Hormuz Messaging Is Deterrence Signaling, Not a Closure Threat".
- **Format:** Sentence case. No clickbait, no question-mark titles unless the post is explicitly an open question.

## 2. BLUF (first block)

- **Rule:** 3 sentences or fewer. Must contain: (a) principal judgment, (b) confidence level, (c) why it matters.
- **Test:** If a reader stops after the BLUF, do they have the assessment? If no, fail.
- Do not open with background, the question you investigated, or the hypotheses you tested.

## 3. Confidence lexicon (fixed, no improvisation)

| Term | Probability |
|---|---|
| Almost certain | 93-99% |
| Very likely | 85-92% |
| Likely | 70-84% |
| Probable | 55-69% |
| Roughly even | 45-54% |
| Unlikely | 15-44% |
| Very unlikely | 3-14% |
| Remote | 1-2% |

- **Banned:** "100%", "0%", "certain", "impossible", "definitely".
- **Required form:** `We assess with [low/moderate/high] confidence that it is [term] that...`
- One confidence term per judgment. Do not stack ("very likely and almost certain").

## 4. Source notes (internal, not published)

- Tag every key claim in the draft with two independent ratings:
  - **Reliability (A-F):** A = highly reliable, F = cannot be judged.
  - **Credibility (1-6):** 1 = confirmed by other sources, 6 = cannot be judged.
- **Test:** Can the editor see whether the principal judgment rests on A1 reporting or C3 chatter? If not, fail.
- Do not let a high-reliability source launder low-credibility information. The axes are independent.

## 5. Alternatives

- **Rule:** Every causal or predictive post names at least one competing explanation and says why the evidence favors your judgment.
- **Minimum:** one sentence, footnoted or inline.
- **Test:** Would a skeptical analyst say you only looked for confirmation? If yes, fail.

## 6. Gaps and what would change this

- **Required block, every post:**
  - What we do not know.
  - What reporting or collection would resolve it.
  - The specific development that would force a revision.
- **Test:** Is "what would change this" concrete enough to be falsifiable? "More information" is a fail; "a verified IRGC exercise order for a live-fire closure drill" is a pass.

## 7. Outlook

- **Rule:** Close with implications and what to watch, not recommendations, unless the format is explicitly advisory.
- Attach a confidence term to any forward-looking claim.
- **Test:** Does the reader know what to monitor next, and how confident you are?

## 8. Voice and attribution

- Collective: "We assess...". Single-bylined posts say so explicitly.
- **Banned:** "I think", "I feel", "in my opinion", "it seems to me".
- Named bylines on every post. No anonymous authorship.
- No first-person speculation presented as analysis.

## Pre-publish gate (run in order)

1. [ ] Title states a judgment or "so what"
2. [ ] BLUF is 3 sentences or fewer with judgment + confidence + implication
3. [ ] Every likelihood term is from the fixed lexicon
4. [ ] No 100%/0%, no "definitely", no "impossible"
5. [ ] Key claims carry internal reliability/credibility tags
6. [ ] At least one alternative explanation addressed
7. [ ] Gaps + "what would change this" block present and falsifiable
8. [ ] Outlook present with confidence language
9. [ ] "We assess" voice; no "I think"; byline present

**Any unchecked box = do not publish.**

## Drafting protocol

- The draft must be exactly as it goes online: the clean article only. No section labels (such as "Article body"), no scaffolding, no verification notes, and no mention of Gemini in public text.
- All other notes (source ratings, gaps, open questions, checks) go in chat or the private Internal Note, never in the post.

## Optional add-ons (not yet adopted)

1. A word-count band per desk (for example signal post 600-900 words, deep dive 1,500-2,500).
2. A corrections log convention: date, claim, original, corrected, reason.
